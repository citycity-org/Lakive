# Lakive Employment Update — August 2026 LFS

Source: Statistics Canada, The Daily, released 2026-09-04 (reference week Aug 9–15). Seasonally adjusted unless noted.
Note: this run occurred Sept 11 (second Friday); the release date was Sept 4. Next LFS: October 9, 2026.

## Headline

- **Unemployment Rate:** 6.4% (— 0.0 pts vs July; ▼ 0.7 pts YoY)
- **Net Jobs:** −41,700 (−0.2%) — first decline after +181,000 Apr–Jul; +216,500 YoY (+1.0%)
- **Youth Unemployment (15–24):** 12.9% (▲ 0.3 pts vs July; ▼ 1.4 pts YoY)
- **Participation Rate:** 65.0% (▼ 0.1 pts)
- **Employment Rate:** 60.8% (▼ 0.1 pts)
- **Full-time:** 17,325,700 (−35,900; −0.2%) · **Part-time:** 3,847,400 (−5,800; −0.2%)
- **Wages:** avg hourly $37.02, +2.0% YoY — slowest since Nov 2017 (was +2.8% in July)
- **Key Sector:** Strongest — Manufacturing (+22,000; +1.2%, mostly Ontario). Weakest — Business/building/support services (−20,000; −2.8%)
- **Insight:** The spring hiring streak has stalled, but the unemployment rate is holding and YoY employment is still positive; relocation decisions should weigh Alberta (unemployment falling, +3.6% jobs YoY) and Manitoba (5.0%) against softening in Quebec/Montréal and BC. Wage growth at 2.0% means cost-of-living comparisons matter more than salary uplift this cycle.

## Flags vs prior month

1. Employment turned negative (−42k) after four months of gains; consensus expected +15k.
2. Youth unemployment reversed course (▲0.3 to 12.9%) after falling 1.7 pts Apr–Jul.
3. BC shift from full-time to part-time (−26k FT / +21k PT) and unemployment up 0.3 — watch for City Compare rankings.
4. Public sector shedding jobs three months running (−78k since May) — relevant to Ottawa/Victoria/Quebec City job pages.
5. Wage growth decelerated sharply (3.3% → 2.8% → 2.0% over three months); bottom-quartile wages +1.1% only.
6. Quebec is the only province with YoY job losses; Montréal drove the August decline.
7. Alberta remains the strongest YoY labour market (+3.6%) despite a flat month.
8. Long-term unemployed share 24.0% — still well above 2017–19 average (17.1%).
